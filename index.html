The Asymmetric Dig-Once Problem

SURFACE: Road Network 25.2M m² | 12,837 units

Resurfacing asphalt or rebuilding foundations does NOT touch the sewer pipe beneath.

⬇️
DIG-ONCE COUPLING RULE:
Excavating a sewer pipe DESTROYS the road above, forcing full road reconstruction (and a Stranded Asset Penalty if the pavement was still new).
SUBSURFACE: Gravity Sewers 2,676.6 km | 841 SSUs

📊 Greedy Baseline — Priority-Based Heuristic Results

Notebook 06 · Operational Rule-of-Thumb

Algorithm: Each year, the model ranks all remaining Sewer Subsystems (SSUs) by urgency (years past due date) and greedily fills the 40 km/year capacity from most urgent to least, permitting fractional splitting across years. Once an SSU finishes, linked roads are forced to renew (Dig-Once). Independently expiring roads wait only if their underlying sewer is due within 5 years; otherwise, they pave immediately.

Section A · Data Quality & Standardization Challenges

Phase 1 Engineering Lessons

What We Found in Raw Municipal Data

  • Missing Pipe Ages: 42.3% of Sewer Subsystems lacked installation years; imputed using soil-type medians to prevent solver bias.
  • GIS Topological Noise: Over 196,000 raw street polygons had overlaps, gaps, and fragmented attributes—standardized into 12,837 intersection-bounded Street Units.
  • Scattered Spreadsheets: Pipe metadata, surface materials, and replacement unit rates lived in disconnected Excel tables without shared foreign keys.
  • Uniform Sewer Unit Rate: Sewers currently use a single citywide flat rate (€975/m + 35% ops); pipe diameter, depth, and material differentiation were unavailable.

Section B · Critical Modeling & Validation Insights

1. The β Calibration Discovery

With an initial overdue penalty of β = 2%/yr, the MILP mathematically proved that paying small fines for 50 years (51% PV) was cheaper than investing 100% CapEx—causing the solver to voluntarily abandon 2,628 km of pipes!

Raising β = 10% and adding exponential decay (γ = 1.05) restored realistic risk incentives above the 4.3% capital recovery threshold. Takeaway: Municipal risk calibration is the model's single highest-leverage parameter.

2. Eliminating End-of-Horizon Bias

An independent model audit revealed that without a Terminal Salvage Value, any finite 50-year optimizer stops building in the 2060s because paying a few years of penalties costs less than a brand-new 40-year asset.

Crediting discounted remaining life at 2074 ensures Rotterdam leaves durable infrastructure heritage rather than gaming the simulation cutoff.

3. The 40 km/yr Capacity Bottleneck

Because 100% of Rotterdam's 2,676.6 km network expires before 2074, a 40 km/yr cap leaves an unavoidable 676.6 km deficit:
