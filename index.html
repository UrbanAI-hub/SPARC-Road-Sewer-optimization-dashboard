Rotterdam Infrastructure Optimizer
Joint Research Initiative · 2025–2074
Convergence Alliance Research · Rotterdam Pilot

Synchronizing Subsurface & Surface Capital Works to Eliminate Premature Pavement Destruction

A mathematical optimization framework developed in partnership with the Municipality of Rotterdam (Gemeente Rotterdam), TU Delft, and Erasmus University Rotterdam. By coordinating 50-year capital renewal windows between gravity sewer subsystems and public street networks, this model prevents unnecessary street excavation, reduces public disruption, and guarantees compliance with contractor capacity ceilings.

The Structural Capacity Bottleneck: Rotterdam maintains 2,676.6 km of gravity sewers, but annual municipal civil works capacity is capped at 40 km/year.
Structural Deficit: 676.6 km (25.3%)
40 km/yr × 50 yrs = 2,000 km Funded Capacity (74.7%)

Policy Significance: Under a fixed 40 km/year contractor capacity, completely renewing the network requires 66.9 years. An average delay of 16.9 years beyond theoretical lifespan is mathematically unavoidable. Determining which assets must be renewed first and synchronizing them with surface repaving is an imperative that manual heuristics cannot resolve without multi-billion euro liabilities.

Heuristic Baseline — Priority-Ranked Operational Simulation

Rule-of-Thumb Practice: 5-Year Dig-Once Window + Urgency Sorting
Current Municipal Baseline

Operational Workflow: Each year, the municipality sorts all remaining Sewer Subsystems (SSUs) by urgency (years overdue past expiration) and fills the 40 km/year civil works quota. Roads directly above an excavated sewer are forced to renew simultaneously (Dig-Once). Independently expiring roads wait if their underlying pipe is scheduled within 5 years; otherwise, they are repaved immediately.

MILP Mathematical Optimizer — Global Lifecycle Coordination

Mixed-Integer Linear Programming via PuLP / CBC Solver
Strict Capacity Ceiling Guaranteed

By solving a Generalized Assignment Model over the entire 50-year horizon, the optimizer evaluates all 42,891 potential assignment combinations (841 SSUs × 51 choices). It globally synchronizes trenching to minimize total discounted expenditure, eliminates premature paving destruction, and enforces strict contractor capacity caps (≤ 40,000 m/year) for every single calendar year.

The Municipal Business Case: Investing in Asset Data Infrastructure

High-ROI Investment Strategy for the Municipality of Rotterdam
Estimated ROI: >40x

Current Data Quality Gaps Identified:

  • Sewer Pipe Installation Ages: 42.3% (356/841) missing. Currently imputed using median soil degradation rates.
  • Road Pavement Construction Dates: 14.8% (1,901/12,837) missing. Currently assigned default 40-year lifespans.
  • Spatial Association Gaps: 8.6% (1,110 street units) lie outside formalized gravity sewer boundaries.
The Investment Case: A targeted €2.5 Million multi-year program consisting of georeferenced CCTV sewer inspections and AI pavement condition scanning would eliminate empirical safety buffers, unlocking an estimated €100M+ in additional optimization efficiency—representing an extraordinary 40x return on investment for Rotterdam.

Four-Phase Municipal Implementation Roadmap

Institutional Integration Pathway
Phase 1 · Pilot

Model Calibration

Refine failure penalty parameters (β, γ) with empirical disruption and traffic diversion records from Rotterdam.

Phase 2 · Systems

Enterprise GIS Integration

Connect optimization outputs directly into municipal asset databases (IBIS / ArcGIS) for maintenance crew dispatching.

Phase 3 · Multi-Utility

Drinking Water & Heating

Coordinate trenching schedules with Evides (drinking water) and district heating networks to split civil costs across 3+ utilities.

Phase 4 · Operations

Rolling Horizon Updates

Deploy annual rolling-horizon re-optimization that adapts dynamically to budget fluctuations and political priorities.
